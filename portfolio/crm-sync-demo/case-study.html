Case study

A real-time sync between two Salesforce orgs

Every new opportunity and its related records, copied across the moment it is created, with each record matched, cleaned and de-duplicated on the way.

The problem

A global software company ran two Salesforce orgs with different data models, picklists, record types and ownership. A deal created in one org had to appear in the other, along with its account, contacts, partner contacts and campaign. A straight copy would have broken on every mismatch, so each record needed to be looked up, translated and matched first.

What I built

An event-driven pipeline in Zapier with JavaScript, split into seven stages. Each stage finds a record first, then updates or creates it, and hands its result to the next.

StageWhat it does
1. TriggerFires on a new deal, waits five minutes so the related records finish saving, then runs the other six stages in order.
2. AccountFits the name to a downstream length limit, maps region with a currency fallback, bands company size, then finds, updates or creates the account.
3. ContactPicks the best contact through a three-tier fallback, matches by email and creates if missing.
4 and 5. Partner contactsPrimary and secondary partner contacts, with early exits for deals that do not need them.
6. CampaignSkips blank values, translates the campaign type between the two systems and links the contact.
7. OpportunityThe largest stage: maps record types, decides new business or upsell from deal history, applies partner rules, guards against duplicates, logs problems to a sheet and creates the deal with its contact roles.

The hard parts

  • Timing. The source org writes related records after the deal. A deliberate wait stops later stages from searching for records that do not exist yet.
  • Missing and messy data. Blank fields, unmapped picklist values and odd names are handled with fallbacks, or left blank on purpose, never guessed.
  • Rules with exceptions. Partner-sourced deals follow one path, but specific campaigns override that rule. Both are encoded and documented.
  • No silent failures. Duplicates and problems are written to an error log instead of disappearing.

The follow-up audit

I checked the integration's documentation against all seven live pipeline exports. About ten mapping rows were wrong, one object was missing from the documentation entirely, and four live fields were undocumented. I catalogued 13 numbered defects, including data that never reached the target system, and wrote up a corrected version of the mapping document.

Tools

Salesforce (two orgs), Zapier (chained sub-zaps), JavaScript code steps, SOQL, Excel for the error log.
